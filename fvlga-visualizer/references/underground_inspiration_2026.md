## 1. OsamaSon, "ik what you did last summer" (STILLS+TEXT)

Credits and text: the official upload is on OsamaSon's own channel, released 2024-10-02. Press (Intersect, OurGeneration) calls it glitchy synths and a "meditative, atmospheric visual". A Genius page notes the video was shot in the same location as the song's era. Directed by Kai Cranmore per the Nettspend press (he also directs OsamaSon).

Seen in stills (verified at still level):
- Grade/colour: a saturated mustard-yellow backdrop (about #E8A817) with a motion-blurred subject. A second look is a solarised, inverted red-orange duotone (roughly #E8602C against pale cream). Third is a cool grey-blue seaside frame. Palette changes by section, not per shot.
- Typography: a ragged torn-paper strip of tiny black book prose pasted over the bottom-left of the yellow frame. A credits card reads in a lime-green (about #D8F060) hand-script plus a heavy sans, over a sea-cliff plate. Small low-contrast subtitle line at frame bottom in the sea shot.
- Emphasis: the credit names are the big words; the book prose is deliberately unreadable-small and works as texture.
- Overlays: pixel-mosaic censor squares beside a face, stacked and repeated strips of the same frame (3 in a column with white gutters), letterbox bars.
- Footage: a locked or slow-moving portrait, one location, shot with a very shallow depth.

INFERRED/UNMEASURED: cut rate (estimate 8 to 14 cuts per 30 s, from the playbook's description of hard cuts and beat flashes), transitions (hard cuts with the sticker layer popping on/off).

Underground vs polished: the paper scrap, mosaic squares and clashing colour sections feel hand-made; the clean, colour-managed plates and credit card are the polish.

ONE idea to borrow: the paper-scrap text. A ragged-edged white strip of tiny serif prose pasted over a colour plate.
Implementation: in the canvas layer, build a polygon with random edge jitter (+/- 2 px per vertex, 30 vertices per side), fill #F4F1E8, add a 2 px drop shadow, rotate -1.5 to 2 degrees, draw body text at about 1.6% of frame height in a free serif (Libre Baskerville or EB Garamond), then multiply-blend a paper-noise texture. Export as a PNG with alpha and overlay in ffmpeg (`overlay=x:y:enable='between(t,a,b)'`). Hold 1 to 3 s so it reads as an object, not a caption.

## 2. 2hollis, "jeans" (STILLS+TEXT)

Credits: the description reads "dir. 2hollis, camera. finn sigil, edit. 2hollis, song prod/written/mix+master. 2hollis". One person writing, directing and editing. Released 2023-10-27, so it is older than the two-year window; kept because it is the cleanest example of no-budget deadpan.

Seen in stills: a bathroom or bedroom mirror shot, a bookshelf behind the subject, a hard point-source flash producing a white-blue lens streak at about 30 degrees, a night exterior with another blown-out flash and a faint green bokeh ghost, a desaturated warm-grey and blue-grey overall grade, heavy low-resolution softness and compression.

- Typography: none visible in the stills. (UNMEASURED whether there are on-screen words.)
- Overlays: none; the flash flare is the whole graphic device.
- Footage: handheld phone or compact-camera, mirror reflections, subject looking at the lens with glowing eyes (flash catching the pupils).
- Cut rate, transitions: UNMEASURED. INFERRED from the genre: slow, few cuts.

Underground vs polished: everything reads as accidental (flare, soft focus, bad exposure), which is exactly what a polished video wouldn't allow.

ONE idea to borrow: a procedural flash flare as the only graphic. Implementation: canvas radial gradient (white core, blue-cyan 8 percent ring) plus a thin rotated linear-gradient streak line across the frame, drawn at a fixed point per shot; ffmpeg `blend=all_mode=screen`. On beat hits, ramp opacity 0 to 1 in 1 frame and decay over 6 frames. Add `eq=contrast=1.1:saturation=0.7` and a light `noise=alls=12:allf=t` for the low-res feel.

Credits and text: premiered 2026-03-09; directed by Kai Cranmore, shot in the snowy woods of Virginia (Hashtag Magazine, Wikipedia). Press describes corpse-painted friends, fireworks, snowballs, donuts, setting fires, and mailboxes smashed with a spiked bat from a pickup bed.

Seen in stills: a cold teal-blue snow forest with a low-contrast filmic grade (shadows about #1B3040, snow about #C8D6DC); a subject with long hair across the face, layered chain and cross; a macro of red lips with blood at the nostril; a night shot against pickup taillights (red on black); a driver-side-car shot of a hand on the face. 2.39:1-style letterboxing is not confirmed; stills look near 16:9.

- Typography: none in stills. UNMEASURED.
- Footage: a mix of beauty close-ups, wide pickup-bed action and in-car coverage. This is crew-made cinema, not DIY.
- Cut rate, transitions: UNMEASURED. INFERRED: moderate (about 10 to 16 per 30 s).

Underground vs polished: the grade and coverage are polished; the content (violence, face paint, teens) and the hard red-on-black moments keep it underground.

ONE idea to borrow: a single colour-contrast accent. Cold teal for 90 percent of the video, with rare saturated-red macro inserts. Implementation: grade everything with ffmpeg `colorbalance=bs=.08:ms=.04:gh=.03` plus `eq=saturation=0.8`; for inserts, `curves` or `colorchannelmixer` to push reds. Schedule 3 to 5 red inserts at 0.5 to 1 s each, placed on the snare.

## 4. xaviersobased, "patchmade" (STILLS+TEXT)

- Typography: none in stills. UNMEASURED.
- Overlays: none visible; the videographer credit lives in the title.
- Cut rate, transitions: UNMEASURED. INFERRED from the one-location stills: very few cuts, a continuous take or two.

Underground vs polished: handheld wide lens at arm's length, clipped sky, no grade. Nothing is hidden.

ONE idea to borrow: the one-location, one-take rule. Implementation: for a section, use a single plate and fake the fisheye on our own footage with ffmpeg `lenscorrection=k1=-0.6:k2=-0.2` (barrel) plus a 1.15x zoom crop; add a slow handheld wobble by `crop` with an expression like `x='20+8*sin(t*1.7)':y='12+6*cos(t*2.3)'`. Keep to 1 to 2 cuts per 30 s in that section so the following high-cut section hits harder.

A free, rights-safe stand-in for any found-footage texture.

## 6. Ken Carson, "Lord Of Chaos (Official Music Video)" (STILLS+TEXT)

Credits and text: directed (and edited, per RateYourMusic) by Nick Spiders; premiered 2025-04-14; the album is More Chaos (Opium/Interscope). Uproxx, AntiMusic and Hype Magazine all describe the same thing: Ken performs in the back of a moving box truck, with strobe lights and a chaotic assortment of objects, "disorienting".

Seen in stills: a narrow, nearly square pillarboxed frame (a VEVO watermark sits bottom-left, a platform artefact, not a design choice); a blown-out white strobe frame with a hooded figure raising an arm; a large red lower-case "warning" title card on black with three lines of small white body text (a photosensitivity notice, INFERRED from the shape; the text is illegible at this size); a close macro of red/pink textured material; a wide exterior of a lit box truck at night.

- Typography: the warning card has a huge red lowercase word and tiny white paragraph text. Emphasis is by scale contrast alone.
- Footage: a single claustrophobic location (the truck) and strobe, so the lighting does the editing.
- Cut rate, transitions: UNMEASURED. INFERRED: the strobe acts as the cut, so the real cut count may be modest while perceived cutting is very high.

Underground vs polished: the warning card and the strobe-only lighting feel like a horror-show flyer. The label watermark and the clean white-blown exposure are the polish.

ONE idea to borrow: the fake "warning" card at the start. Implementation: 2 s of black with one huge red lowercase word at about 18% of frame height and 3 to 4 lines of tiny white sans (about 1.5%), and no music for the first half second. Then smash into the first drop. Canvas draws the card; ffmpeg `concat` or `enable=between` handles it; use `adelay` to leave a short silence.

## Cross-cutting notes

- None of the six has big flashing kinetic type in its stills. Text in these references is rare and small (the paper strip, a credit card, a warning card), so scale contrast carries the emphasis. Do not assume big captions are the only underground move.
- Three of six lean on one cold or one saturated colour per section (yellow, teal, magenta-teal). This agrees with the playbook's "the grade is the brand".
- The two older videos (jeans 2023, patchmade 2022) are chosen for look, not recency, and are flagged as such.

## Top 10 borrowable devices

1. Paper-scrap text strip: ragged white paper polygon with tiny serif prose, slight rotation, drop shadow, paper-noise multiply (ref 1).
2. Palette-per-section grading: swap the whole grade (yellow, duotone, cold grey-blue) at section boundaries instead of mixing colours in one shot (ref 1, 3).
3. Procedural flash flare: radial core plus streak line, screen-blended, fired on the beat (ref 2).
4. Single accent insert: a cold teal base with short, saturated red macro inserts on snares (ref 3).
5. Fake fisheye plus handheld wobble on our own footage for a one-location documentary section (ref 4).
6. Procedural sticker/graffiti wall as the plate behind a cutout artist, magenta/teal unified (ref 5).
7. The "warning" card: black, one giant lowercase red word, tiny white body text, silence before the drop (ref 6).
8. Pixel-mosaic censor squares over a face or an object, stepped in size over 4 to 6 frames (ref 1).
9. Triple-stack frame repeat: the same frame in 3 stacked strips with white gutters, on a hook word (ref 1).
10. Strobe-as-edit: instead of more cuts, drive 1-frame white/black flashes (`eq=brightness` expression gated by beat times) so a static plate feels like it is cutting (ref 6).
